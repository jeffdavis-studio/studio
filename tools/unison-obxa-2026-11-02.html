Tap again to stop.

Eight voices. Play chords on the keys, several at once, or type A W S E D F T G Y H U J K; Z / X shift octaves. Each new note goes to the next of the eight voice cards in turn, as on the machine, and every card is slightly different (voice slop). Touch sets how hard the keys play. The OB-Xa's 2-pole / 4-pole filter switch belongs to the patch: 2-pole is open and brassy, 4-pole rounder with less top.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

No freely licensed recording of a real OB-Xa exists, so the reference pack is nine excerpts of its successor, a real Oberheim OB-8 (same voice-card lineage, same filter chip with the 2/4-pole switch), from two players on freesound (CC0 / CC-BY), each checked by measurement before use.

Moved, on all four patches: the second oscillator's detune. Every chord in the pack, from both players, has its two oscillators per voice 12 to 19 cents apart, read directly as split peaks in the spectrum. Brass 9 → 17 cents, pad 12 → 18, comp 7 → 11, stab 10 → 15. That is how the machine is played, not one player's patch.

The filter: the same player uses both modes (a closed pad and a single note read 4-pole; a detuned chord reads 2-pole), so the switch is a patch choice. Since 2026-10-05 it is one: every archetype in the book sets its own pole, and every part has both kinds.
