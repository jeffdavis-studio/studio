A shaker is played back and forth. The forward stroke is the strong one: the hand stops and the beads slam into the far wall together. The back stroke is the return: softer, slower, and the beads arrive a hair late. How hard you play matters: click high on a pad for a soft stroke and low for a hard one, or type F (forward) and J (back), which use the Touch slider. Alternate F and J to shake it yourself.

Four bars of one of the song engine's own shaker patterns (running sixteenths, eighths, or the off-beat sixteenths), played through the instrument exactly the way a song does it. Straight is the pattern as written: every stroke on the grid at the same strength, the hand simply going back and forth. Played is the same four bars through the session player: sitting a touch ahead of the beat, leaning on the off-beats or the downbeats depending on the style, no two strokes quite the same, the level drifting a little over the bars, and on the funkier styles the odd bar where the hand turns around. Each song draws its own player; this one is a fixed example.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is recordings of real hand-played instruments from freesound: plastic egg shakers (a forward-and-back pair, a soft and a hard stroke, a live groove in sixteenths and a roll), a big pair of maracas (one shake, and both played in time), and a cabasa (a strike on the palm, a played figure and one long turn of the handle). Every file was checked by measurement before use.

Moved, from the real instruments: the overall color of each one, how fast a stroke's rattle dies, how quickly a stroke comes up, how much quieter the back stroke sits in a groove, and how much louder and brighter a hard stroke is than a soft one. The biggest single change: real shakers, maracas and cabasas have almost nothing in the low range, far less than my first build, so the bead sound now rolls off steeply below the treble.

Still loose, on the record: the maracas' measurements rest on one shake, and a shake is a random cloud of seeds, so their ring-out is set by direction more than precision. The cabasa recordings disagree with each other, so its metal rings stay where they were set by ear. No tube shaker passed the checks; the shaker is fitted to egg shakers.
