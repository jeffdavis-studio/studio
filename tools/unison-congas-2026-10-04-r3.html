Pick a stroke, then hit a pad. Open lets the head ring at its note. Slap is the crack: the hand's edge snaps on and stays, so there is almost no note, just the bite. Mute is the open stroke with the hand left on the head, a short closed tone. Touch is the heel or the fingertips resting in, the quiet notes that fill a groove. How hard you play matters: tap high on a pad for a soft stroke and low for a hard one, or type F (high drum) and J (low drum), which use the Touch slider; 1-4 pick the stroke.

Open, slap, mute and touch on every drum, one tap each. The selected instrument plays through the panel; the other at center.

A four-bar phrase of one of the song engine's own hand-drum parts (the tumbao, the single hit, or the two hits that close a phrase), played through the instrument the way a song does it. Straight is the part as written: every hit an open tone, on the grid, at the same strength. Played is the same phrase through the session player: the slap on two and the low drum's open tone on four, closed tones where the hand sits, quiet heel-and-tip touches between the written hits, a touch ahead of or behind the beat, no two hits quite the same, and the odd bar played a little differently. Each song draws its own player; this one is a fixed example.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is recordings of real hand-played drums from freesound: a set of congas (an open tone on each of the three drums, slaps, the heel and fingertip notes of a tumbao, and a few bars of a played groove) and two pairs of bongos (open tones on both drums, loud and soft, a slap, the heel-and-tip strokes, and a slowly played martillo). Every file was checked by measurement before use.

Moved, from the real drums: the tuning of every drum, how long each one rings, the color of the open tone (which overtones it carries and how strong), how a slap cracks and how fast it dies, how quiet and short the heel and fingertip notes are, and on the bongos how much brighter a hard stroke is than a soft one. Two changes to the model itself came out of the measuring: the real heads carry overtones well above anything my first build had, so the drum now has its upper overtones, and a real slap stops the whole head, not just its low note.

Still loose, on the record: no recording of a plain muted tone passed the checks, so the mute is set by ear. The bongos' two drums have different overtone patterns and the model shares one set between them, so the bongos' color is the closer of the two but not exact. The two conga slap recordings disagree about how long a slap lasts, so the slap sits between them.
