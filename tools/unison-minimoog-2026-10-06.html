Unison — the Minimoog

The mono voice — the bass, and since October 5 the lead, hook riff and arpeggio as well (the Juno still plays most of those) — synthesized live in your browser from src/instruments/minimoog.js, the same file the song render calls. Nothing here is a sample. The patch book: every archetype a song can draw, grouped by the part it plays. The decay switch is part of each patch: on, a note rings out on its decay time when the key lifts; off, it stops. Each patch opens at its base setting; Draw a variation moves its knobs inside the range a song draws from, the way a song does.

One voice, last-note priority, the way the Model D plays: hold a key and press another and the pitch glides there without re-striking the contours; let go first and the next note strikes fresh. Tap the keys or type A W S E D F T G Y H U J K, Z / X to shift octaves. Touch sets how hard the keys play: it opens the filter as well as the level. Bass line (Lead line on the lead, counter and arp patches, two octaves up) plays a two-bar line through the voice's own schedule(), the entry point the song uses.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is seven recordings of a real Minimoog (freesound, CC0 / CC-BY), each checked by measurement before use. It confirmed the circuit: the filter falls faster than any 12 dB filter can, so it is a 24 dB ladder, and the release darkens with the decay switch on, as ours does. One real recording's four-second filter sweep is traced by this model to within 1.5 dB/oct.

The patch book (October 6): 28 patches, the four bass families (the drawn bass line picks the family, as before) and the lead, counter and arpeggio parts, the decay switch set per patch, half on and half off. The two fat basses carry the fit below; the rest are voiced by ear against written targets for brightness, attack, decay and release tail. Each is level-matched, K-weighted, to the patch its part played before, on that part's real song lines, so the strip trims hold. New on the voice: osc 3 as a modulation source (the mod wheel sending a slow osc 3 to pitch or filter), used by the vibrato lead and off everywhere else.

Lead and riff (October 5): the Minimoog now plays some songs' lead, counter-melody and arpeggio, with the Juno still on most of them. lead rings out with the decay switch on and glides between held notes; riff has the switch off, so every note plucks. Both are voiced by ear, not fitted, because the reference recordings are single held notes with no lead line in them. Each is trimmed to the loudness of the Juno patch it replaces on that strip.

Moved, on the fat patch only: VCO 3 (one octave down) sits 12.6 dB under the note, as in a real Minimoog open bass (level 0.55 → 0.23), and VCO 2 is detuned 1 cent rather than 7, because a real "deep detuned" Minimoog bass beats about a quarter as hard as 7 cents does.
