drum machine

The studio drum machine the pop songs lean on, built from recordings of the real machine's chips. Every pad here is one hit straight off the machine at its center settings. Listen for the weight of the kick against the snare, how dry and short the hats are, and how the little percussion (conga, cowbell, clave, tambourine, cabasa) sits underneath. Play is the pattern the page opens on, looped twice.

bass guitar

An electric bass built as a plucked string, a pickup and an amp, with the tone sitting well back the way a passive bass straight into the desk does. A soft note is round and quiet, a hard one brighter and grabs. Straight is the pattern as written; played is the same bars through the session player: a little ahead of or behind the beat, no two notes the same strength, the odd ghost note and now and then a slide. The line follows the voicing: syncopated on finger and slap, octaves on pick, straight eighths on muted.

shakers

A session percussionist's shaker, maracas and cabasa: beads in a shell, each stroke throwing them against the walls. The forward stroke is the strong one, the beads slamming into the far wall together; the back stroke is the softer return, the beads arriving a hair late. Played leans on the off-beats or the downbeats, no two strokes quite the same, the level drifting a little over the bars.

congas and bongos

Hand drums: a skin head that rings in its own set of tones, struck by a hand. Open lets the head ring at its note; slap is the crack, almost no note, just the bite; mute is the open stroke with the hand left on, a short closed tone; touch is the quiet heel and fingertip notes that fill a groove. The congas are the conga, the higher quinto and the low tumba; the bongos are the macho and the hembra. Straight is every hit an open tone; played adds the slap, the closed tones and the touches in between.

rhythm guitar

A session player's clean electric rhythm guitar: steel strings struck with a pick, a bright single pickup, a compressor and a light chorus, straight into the desk. Down strums low to high and lets the chord ring; up comes back lighter on fewer strings; chuck is the dry funk scratch with no note; stab is the chord struck and cut short. The sounds here are the full chord; the grooves use the small top-string shape funk lives on. Played slips muted scratches between the chords, lots in funk and few elsewhere.
