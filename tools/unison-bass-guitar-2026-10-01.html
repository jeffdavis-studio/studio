The electric bass, synthesized live in your browser from src/instruments/bassguitar.js, the same file the song render calls: a plucked-string model, four strings, a pickup and an amp. Nothing here is a sample.

Low E to the G two octaves up, the whole neck. Click the keys or type A W S E D F T G Y H U J K; Z / X shift octaves. Each string plays one note at a time, as on the instrument: a new note on the same string stops the last one, and notes on different strings can ring together. How hard you play matters: a soft note is round and quiet, a hard one is brighter and grabs. Click high on a key for a soft note and low on a key for a hard one; typed notes use the Touch slider.

Four bars of one of the song engine's own bass patterns, played through the voice exactly the way a song does it. Straight is the pattern as written, every note on the grid at its written strength, the way a synth bass plays it. Played is the same four bars through the session player: a little ahead of or behind the beat, no two notes quite the same strength, notes a touch longer or shorter, the odd ghost note in the gaps, and now and then a slide into a note. Each song draws its own player; this one is a fixed example. The pattern follows the voicing: a syncopated line on finger and slap, octaves on pick, straight eighths on muted.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is recordings of real electric basses from freesound (CC0 and CC-BY): one player's short-scale bass recorded straight in, eight notes deep from soft to hard, low E up to the D an octave and a half above; a picked note on another bass; and a long open E on a P-style bass left to ring for sixteen seconds. Every file was checked by measurement before use. One, sold as a slap note, turned out not to be a single clean string and was thrown out.

Moved, from the real bass: how stiff the strings are (the overtones run slightly sharp, more so up the neck), how long a note rings and how fast its upper overtones die away, how bright the attack is and how much brighter it gets when you dig in, and the slow two-speed fade of a long note. The biggest single change was the tone: a real bass straight into the desk is much darker in its body than my first build, so the electronics now work the way a passive bass's do, a tone control on top of the pickup's own resonance, and the tone sits well back.

Still off, on the record for the next pass: the open low strings are brighter in their body than the real one, and nothing on the panel closes that, so it is the model and not a setting. The picked attack is a little softer than the real picked note.

Voiced, not measured: slap and palm-muted. No clean recording of either on a real bass turned up, so these are built from how the instrument is played, not fitted.
