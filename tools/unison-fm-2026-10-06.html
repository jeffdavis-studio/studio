Unison — FM keys

The six-operator FM voice, synthesized live in your browser from src/instruments/fm.js, the same file the song render calls. Nothing here is a sample. The patch book: every patch a song can draw, grouped by the part it plays (electric piano and keys on the comp, glass and mallets on the section hits, brass on the offbeats, and the bass). Each opens at its base setting; Draw a variation moves its knobs inside the range a song draws from, the way a song does.

One chord per part (one low note for the bass), soft or hard, on the selected patch when it plays that part, otherwise on the part's house patch. Phrase plays four bars of a progression from the song engine's harmony book through the voice's own schedule(); tap again to stop.

Sixteen voices. Play chords on the keys, several at once, or type A W S E D F T G Y H U J K; Z / X shift octaves. The touch range is narrow on purpose. On every keys patch the whole range, Soft to Hard, sits in a narrow band near where the old soft setting was, so every note is round and a hard one is only a little louder and brighter than a soft one: consistency over range. The house brass was already narrow and keeps its own; the new brass patches get the narrow band too. The bass is not keys. With a mouse, click high on a key for a soft note and low for a hard one; typed notes use the Touch slider. On a touch screen the keys play at the Touch setting.

Tap a playing button again to stop. Four bars of the electric piano phrase above, through the voice exactly the way a song plays it. Straight is the phrase as written, every chord on the grid with all its notes at once, the way a sequencer plays it. Played is the same four bars through the session keys player: a little ahead of or behind the beat, each chord rolled low to high by a few milliseconds the way a hand lands, beat one and the backbeat leaned on, no two bars quite the same strength. In a song the player is always on; each song draws its own, and this one is a fixed example.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is recordings of real Yamaha FM hardware from freesound (CC0 and Sampling Plus): a DX7 electric piano played up the keyboard, DX7 bass notes, and a DX5 (two DX7 engines in one case) played soft to hard and held long. Every file was checked by measurement before use, and three were thrown out for not being what they claimed.

Moved, on the electric piano: how much it darkens up the keyboard. The real one keeps more of its brightness in the upper octaves than my first voicing did, so it now darkens much less as you go up, and the tone is a hair brighter.

Checked and left alone: the bass. Its brightness already sat where the real DX7 bass notes are.

Voiced, not measured: glass and brass. No clean recording of either on real hardware turned up, so these are built from how the instrument works, not fitted.
