The six-operator FM voice, synthesized live in your browser from src/instruments/fm.js, the same file the song render calls. Nothing here is a sample.

Sixteen voices. Play chords: click the keys or type A W S E D F T G Y H U J K, several at once; Z / X shift octaves. How hard you play matters, most of all on the electric piano: a soft note is round, a hard one barks and rings. Click high on a key for a soft note and low on a key for a hard one; typed notes use the Touch slider. Chord progression plays four bars through the voice's own schedule(), the entry point the song uses.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is recordings of real Yamaha FM hardware from freesound (CC0 and Sampling Plus): a DX7 electric piano played up the keyboard, DX7 bass notes, and a DX5 (two DX7 engines in one case) played soft to hard and held long. Every file was checked by measurement before use, and three were thrown out for not being what they claimed.

Moved, on the electric piano: how much it darkens up the keyboard. The real one keeps more of its brightness in the upper octaves than my first voicing did, so it now darkens much less as you go up, and the tone is a hair brighter.

Checked and left alone: the bass. Its brightness already sat where the real DX7 bass notes are.

Voiced, not measured: glass and brass. No clean recording of either on real hardware turned up, so these are built from how the instrument works, not fitted.
