The Sky on walls

What the measurements say

This is your v6 sketch, byte for byte, projected into a room. Every wall is showing the same render — not a panorama, not four crops of one.

That works because of something in your own shader that hadn't been named: The Sky's image is a pure function of height. The gradient and all ten cloud bands are computed from the vertical coordinate alone. The only thing that touches horizontal position is the film grain.

I measured it rather than trusting the read. Across all nine sky states, the largest difference between any two columns of the image is 4/255 — exactly the grain's amplitude — against a vertical range of up to 95/255. The image is a stack of horizontal lines.

What follows from that

The same signal on every wall already is one continuous sky. The requirements sheet treats "unified sky across all surfaces" and "parallel skies per wall" as two options with a preference. They're the same thing. There is no azimuth to reconcile and no content seam at a corner — an aligned corner measures 1/255 of difference, which is below the grain.

No media server. Scale C assumed one, because unified multi-surface content usually needs warping and blending across a shared canvas. Identical content doesn't. One output to a distribution amp feeding N projectors does it. Or N players kept in sync by the deterministic clock you already built — same hash, same seek, same frame.

Wall width is free; wall height is the constraint. A 40 ft wall and a 12 ft wall can carry the same signal and still read as one sky. So for venue hunting, ceiling height and matched heights across surfaces are the filter. Width barely matters. Wide-short media walls are the bad fit — they letterbox a piece that lives in the vertical.

How much alignment error you can absorb

Less obvious, and it went the friendly direction. A drop of D feet on a 12 ft wall shifts color by one gradient step of D/12. Worst case across all six states, at eye level:

aligned1/255gone
3 in off5.6/255invisible
6 in off10.6/255fine
12 in off20.4/255starts to show at dawn
18 in off28.2/255visible — the Misalign button

Grain is ±2/255 for reference. This is a forgiving install. And it's state-dependent: 18 inches is loud at dawn and nearly nothing at dusk, so a survey done at midday will flatter a sloppy alignment.

The part I can't measure

Does a room of four identical vertical gradients read as being inside a sky — or as wallpaper?

Everything above says it's technically one continuous sky. Whether it's perceptually one is your eye, not my arithmetic. That's what the corner view is for. If it reads, Scale C is a much cheaper and much more available thing than the sheet assumes. If it doesn't, one wall done well is the piece and the multi-surface question is closed.

Rooms follow the Three Scales sheet: S 14×12×9 ft, M 26×22×12, L 44×36×18. Eye height 5.5 ft. Sky code untouched.
