The methods, the quantization rule, and what this can't show

0. program default. What the main program, intervals/Intervals_v9.js, plots: current with its black cubic a (and its achromatic a), mix ease b, coverage target, line spacing and black angle, read from that file's plot settings when this page loads (Jeff's 9/29 values: a = 1.2, a = 0 on achromatic tokens, b = 0.1, black 45; target 0.95, spacing 0.45). Change them in the program and this preset follows. The side's boxes show it as cubic + mix ease + target and spacing; moving a slider leaves the preset. URL: ?a=p.
0. program default, b / target / spacing on the sliders. The program with its own a, achromatic a and black angle, and the side's mix ease, target and spacing in place of its own; moving b, target or spacing off the program lands here. The page opens on program default (A) against this with mix ease 0 (B), on 0x8e98544e. URL: ?b=pm&xb=0.

  1. current, black 45. The pushed export: each anchor's two inks on their own grids at 22.5 / 67.5 (start anchor) and 112.5 / 157.5 (end anchor), densities solved so the bar lands on its tone; black on its own grid, turned to 45.
  2. current + black curve 1.85. Same, with black's line count thinned to a footprint of 1 − exp(−1.85 × share), because 0.45 mm black lines cover about 1.85× their share. Only black changes.
  3. cubic a (a black curve on method 1). Black's weight into the density solve becomes s − a·s²·(1 − s) for shade share s; the other inks are untouched, as with the 1.85 curve. a runs 0 to 3 in steps of 0.1 (presets a = 0.6, 1, 2, 3); 3 is the monotonic limit, since the slope 1 − a(2s − 3s²) reaches 0 at s = 1/3 when a = 3, and above that a darker bar would get less black. a = 0 is current; a = 1 is within about 0.01 of the 1.85 curve up to a share of 0.35, then parts from it: the cubic reaches 1 at share 1, so an all-black bar stays black, while the 1.85 curve's applied weight tops out at 0.46 (a footprint of 0.84). Pick a curve for either side in the box under the thumbnail; the plot shows the chosen curve (solid), the diagonal (current), the 1.85 curve (dashed), the token's shaded-bar range (band) and the picked bar (dot); the table under it gives applied at shares 0.1 to 0.9. Footprint under each render is the share of the bar black's own lines cover.
  4. black in the slots. No black grid. Each shaded anchor's two grids are solved on its whole inked share, and black takes the anchor's shade fraction of those grids' lines as a Euclidean rhythm.
  5. screen, round robin. The 9/26 screen model Jeff saw: four equal grids per bar, every pen spread over all four by a smooth weighted round robin. Shares are right on average, but a pen's lines on a grid are unevenly spaced.
  6. screen EVEN. Four grids at one pitch, and every pen on a grid takes one residue class of one stride (every s-th line), so its lines are always exactly evenly spaced. Shares are quantized to what the stride layouts can make.
  7. screen EVEN + bias. The same stride rule with the grids' pitches free. Bias 0 is method 5; bias 1 puts each pen on as few grids as the pen count allows (four pens: one grid each, the overprint; one pen: four grids; black shares the grids its shaded anchor's inks hold).

7. slot fill (Jeff, 2026-09-29). The program default, with empty hatch slots reused. Each bar has four color slots at 22.5 / 67.5 (the interval's start anchor's two inks) and 112.5 / 157.5 (its end anchor's), and black on its own 45 grid. A slot is empty when no ink lays a line on it. Empty slots are handed out one at a time, each to one ink: the ink with the most lines first, then the next, and round again from the densest once every ink has one (ties: the lower pen number). The ink takes the empty angle farthest from the ones it already holds, so a second slot crosses the first near 90°. An ink with extra slots spreads its weight evenly over all of them: every one of its grids gets the same pitch, so it lays the same ink as before and the density solve is untouched; its line count moves a little because the four angles cross a bar at different lengths. Four-ink bars are unchanged line for line.
Scope, by artwork (default): decided once for the token, by angle. The slot angles are the same in every bar, but the inks on them belong to each interval's anchors, so the token-wide question is per angle: an angle that only one ink ever hatches at is that ink's in every bar (where a bar leaves it empty, the ink takes it), and an angle no bar uses is handed out as above, inks ranked by their lines over the whole token. An angle two inks share anywhere belongs to no one, so a token whose intervals mix their pens is unchanged; a monochromatic token's end bars hatch like its middle bars; an achromatic token fills. By bar: each bar decides from its own lines, so the first and last bar of every interval (only the one anchor's two inks) hatch in four directions, and the neighbor bar hands the slots back as the far ink enters.
Achromatic box (default on): where there is no color ink, black leaves 45 and takes the four slot angles, its lines split evenly. Off: black stays on 45. The readout under each render lists every bar's lines by angle, program → fill. URL: ?a=f (or a=7), fa=bar / fb=artwork for each side's scope, ka=0 / kb=0 for the box off, nb=0–3 for the neighbors.

Mix ease (on the current family, with any black curve). Every crossing of two different inks is ink on ink, so a mixed bar can print darker than its digital color. A bar's coverage target becomes target × (1 − b·X), target the side's coverage target (below), with b on the slider (0 to 1, presets 0.3 and 0.6) and X the bar's mix index: color grid f covers cf = min(1, wf·m) in the solve, two grids of different inks cross on cf·cg of the bar, weighted by |sin| of the angle between them, and X = Σ cfcg|sin(θf − θg)| / (F·Z), F the bar's footprint (W × target) and Z the same ratio for four inks balanced at full tone (1.412 at target 0.95), so that bar is 1 and a one-ink bar is 0. Black is left out (its crossings are the black curve's), and two slots of the same ink never count. X grows with tone as well as with mixing: a pale four-ink bar has little ink on ink. The multiplier is then solved again on the lower target, so every grid in the bar thins in proportion, black's included. b = 0 is the export byte for byte. URL: ?a=c&aa=1.2&xa=0.3.

Coverage target and spacing (2026-10-09, on the current family, with any curve and mix ease). Target is what a full bar prints at: a bar of ink share W is solved to cover W × target of the paper, so it moves every bar's ink, black's included, and the mix index's footprint with it. 0.90 to 1.00 in steps of 0.005; the program's is 0.95, its 100%. Spacing is the line pitch of a solid fill, 0.35 to 0.60 mm in steps of 0.01 (the program's 0.45, the nib's width): every grid's line count scales by 0.45 / spacing at the same solved density, so below 0.45 the lines overlap at full tone and above it paper shows between them. Copy settings under each side prints its constants as they go into buildSVG's plot settings, to hand back as they are (blackCubicAchromatic appears only when the side would change it; the 1.85 curve has no setting). URL: ta / tb for target, sa / sb for spacing, e.g. ?b=pm&xb=0.1&tb=0.97.

Luminance. Under each plot, per bar: L* of the plot's mean luminance (sRGB → linear → L*, the bar's whole-mm tiles at 20 px/mm) against L* of the digital render over the same pixels (the bar's one flat color), and Δ = plot − digital, negative = paper darker. Measure all bars adds the whole-token summary (median and worst Δ, color bars X ≥ 0.05 against pure and achromatic ones) and a per-bar Δ chart. The raster model has no pen blot, bleed or feathering, and multiplies inks over paper as the overprint model.

Quantization rule (5 and 6). A grid's lines are split among its pens by one of eleven exact covers: 1; ½ ½; ½ ¼ ¼; ⅓ ⅓ ⅓; ¼ ¼ ¼ ¼; ½ ¼ ⅛ ⅛; ⅓ ⅓ ⅙ ⅙; ½ ⅙ ⅙ ⅙; ¼ ¼ ¼ ⅛ ⅛; ½ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛; ⅓ ⅙ ⅙ ⅙ ⅙. A pen with share 1/s takes lines r, r+s, r+2s… The grid's line count is nudged to the nearest multiple of its largest stride so every class has exactly n/s lines. A pen's strides on its grids must divide one another (pitch P, 2P, 4P, never 2P against 3P). Per bar, the layout is the one with the smallest squared share error, then, by bias, the most spread (bias 0) or the most concentrated (bias 1); sparse strides cost a little. At bias 0 all four grids share one pitch, so the share error is the price: up to about 0.06 on a bar. As bias rises the grid weights float and the error falls to about 0.015 on five-pen bars and 0 on bars of four pens or fewer.

digital. The artwork's own screen render for the hash: the flat bars draw() fills, in the same color (betterLerp, p5's rounded levels), laid into the same composition millimeters as the plot, so at every zoom, in the whole-token view and on any bar crop it is the same pixel size and position as the plot beside it. Pick it for either side, or press ⇄ digital (key d for side A, shift-d for side B) to flip a plot side to the digital render in place and back. Under it: each picked bar's HSB (p5's 0–360 / 0–100 / 0–100), hex and RGB.

Evenness. The bar alone at 20 px/mm; 1 mm tiles of linear luminance (whole mm across the bar, 1 mm dropped at each end); sd and the 5–95 spread over the tiles, the black-angles sheet's measure. Lower is more even. On light bars the line pitch is near 1 mm, so a tile holds one line or two by phase and the number runs high for every method; compare methods on the same bar, not bars with each other.

Can't show. Pen blot at each line's start and stop, paper bleed and feathering, a pen crossing its own lines (drawn as one path, so it never darkens itself; real ink does a little), ink colors beyond the pen table's hex, nib wear, and plotter backlash. Tone is the rendered coverage; the artwork's color is a Lab lerp, so the L* pair shows how far the plot sits from the screen image, not an error in the plot.
