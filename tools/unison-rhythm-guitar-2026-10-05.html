Pick a chord, then hit a stroke. Down strums from the low strings to the high ones and lets the chord ring. Up comes back from the top, lighter, on fewer strings. Chuck is the funk scratch: the fretting hand lies on the strings without pressing, so the pick gets a dry "chk" and no note. Stab is the chord struck and cut short, the reggae skank and the funk stab. Palm is the picking hand's heel resting on the strings at the bridge: a short, thick chug, new wave's eighth notes. Top strings plays the small chord shape high on the neck that funk lives on; full chord adds the low root. How hard you play matters: tap high on a pad for a soft stroke and low for a hard one, or type D, U, C, S, which use the Touch slider; 1-5 pick the chord.

Tap a playing button again to stop. A four-bar phrase of one of the song engine's own guitar parts, on a chord change from its own harmony books, played through the instrument the way a song does it. Straight is the part as written: the hand going down on the beat and up in between, every stroke on the grid. The newer parts: muted sixteenths are the scratch line on its own, every sixteenth a chuck with the beat leaned on; octave melody is two strings an octave apart playing a short tune from the chord's own notes; arpeggio picks the chord one string at a time and lets it ring; palm-muted eighths chug on the low strings. Played is the same phrase through the session player: muted scratches slipped in between the written chords (lots in funk, few elsewhere), accents that lean, the downstrokes a little wider than the ups, a touch ahead of or behind the beat, and the odd bar played a little differently. Each song draws its own player; this one is a fixed example.

What was measured, what moved, and what the reference pack could not tell us

The reference pack is recordings of real clean electric guitars from freesound: a Telecaster strumming open chords and playing straight eighths, a Strat playing reggae skanks, and a ska chord progression. Every file was checked by measurement before use, and two of the strums failed (one too slow and uneven a strum to read, one with a pick spike over its ring), so they were left out of the fit. On 10/05 it grew for the guitars and parts: a Les Paul's open string, a Les Paul's bridge humbucker palm-muting, a single-coil arpeggio and a miked Strat note. No Tele recording names its pickup and no clean octave line turned up, so those are built from how they are made and played.

Moved, from the real guitars: how long the strings ring, how fast the bright top of a strum fades into the ring, how bright the ring is (the bridge pickup's position), how bright the pick's attack is, and how soon a skank is cut. The fit was made with the compressor and chorus switched off, because none of the recordings has them, and the first try with them on showed the compressor letting the pick's attack jump out where the real guitars don't.

Still loose, on the record: the pack has no clean recording of an upstroke, a muted chuck, the small top-string funk shape, a soft and hard pair, or a funk sixteenth part, so those are set by ear: the chuck, the upstroke's lightness and the touch response especially. The compressor and the chorus are the era's, set by ear. The strum spread stays where I set it: the recorded strums are much slower than a funk player's.
